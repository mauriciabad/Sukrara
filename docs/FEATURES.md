# Sukrara behaviour catalog

The features and exact behaviours of every Sukrara app. One line per behaviour, with the rule and the numbers; the
"why" stays in the investigation reports (`docs/NN-*.md`).

Rule: every change that adds or changes a behaviour updates this file in the same commit.

**Complete flows** (CLAUDE.md; user, 2026-10-21: "There's no way to unmute!"): every feature section has a
**States and exits** paragraph: each state a user can enter, where it is visible, and how to change it and leave it
(undo, cancel, turn off, unmute, go back) on every platform and surface that shows it. `tools/check/run.sh` (check
`states`) fails on a section without one unless `tools/check/allow.json` `states_pending` lists it with the reason.

## Alerts

Rules: [poc/spikes/alerts/SPEC.md](../poc/spikes/alerts/SPEC.md); timelines: [poc/spikes/alerts/scenarios](../poc/spikes/alerts/scenarios/).

- Every change to the alert rules adds or updates a scenario in `poc/spikes/alerts/scenarios/*.txt`; Android
  `AlertScenarioTest` and the Mac `-alerts-selftest` both run all of them and must pass (SPEC §7). Mac: the pure
  `SukraraAlerts` engine runs them in `AlertsTests` (`cd apps/macos && swift test --filter AlertsTests`, one test case
  per scenario from `contracts/alerts/scenarios`).
- **LO / HI** (Android and Mac, SPEC §1): a sensor's "below / above my range" reading (`sgv < 40` / `> 400`) matches
  every low / every high kind whatever the thresholds, so the most severe one turned on (very low, extremely high)
  alerts at once instead of waiting for *No glucose data*; never a falling-high prediction from a HI, and a LO / HI is
  never "back in range by the margin" (a Mute / Remind less of that side holds). Alert texts say
  *LO · below 40 mg/dL* / *HI · above 400 mg/dL* (es *por debajo de 40* / *por encima de 400*), never 39 / 401, and
  the Nightscout upload and the xDrip+ endpoint compute no slope from or to one (scenarios `levels.txt`).
- Kinds and defaults (the user's settings): very low < 55 every 5 min (alarm volume), low < 70 every 5 min, high ≥ 180
  every 15 min, very high ≥ 250 every 10 min, extremely high ≥ 300 every 5 min (alarm volume), falling/rising fast
  2 mg/dL/min every 30 min (off), no data after 20 min every 60 min; other kinds at notification volume.
- Lows are strictly below the threshold (70 → alerts at 69); highs at or above it.
- "Very low" (id `very_low`) replaced "urgent low" everywhere (settings JSON, channels, sounds, events); no migration.
- Level alerts: one at a time, the most severe wins; a worse kind of the same side alerts at once; getting better
  (very low → low) doesn't alert.
- Rate alerts (falling/rising fast) run next to level alerts; a reading without a rate (a LO / HI has none) keeps an
  open falling / rising fast as it is (alert up, repeat due, a Mute not back by it): a LO isn't a sign the fall stopped; no data runs on a timer and clears on the next reading. The Android timer is an elapsed-time alarm, so a clock change (`TIME_SET`) re-checks at once and plans it again (round 22: with the clock moved forward the old high stayed up and no "no data" came; a real gap alerts on time, scenario *A high with no more readings…*).
- Readings older than 15 min never alert; the alert clears when the reading gets that old.
- Episodes: one per group and side; a brief exit (back in range for less than the repeat interval, at least 5 min)
  clears the notification but keeps the episode, its repeat count and its treatments; no new "first" alert.
- Repeats count from the last alert of the episode; repeat 0 = once per episode.
- Logging the answering treatment clears the alert; when its mute window ends and it still matches, it alerts at once
  and the repeats count from there.
- Carbs mute very low, low and falling fast (default 15 min); rapid insulin mutes high, very high and rising fast
  (default 60 min); no treatment mutes extremely high or no data (the user's own Mute / Mute all do); window = `t ≤ now < t + min`; deleted entries don't count.
- Notification actions (round f30, SPEC §2; the user 2026-10-21: "two kinds of buttons … Mute 30 min doesn't conceptually belong there"; Android guidance: up to three actions that don't duplicate the tap, developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/build-notification#Actions, choices via RemoteInput): every alert notification, every kind (very low and extremely high included), has exactly **Mute…** and **Remind less** (Mac *Remind Less*) on Android and Mac. Mute… is one action whose choices are the lengths (Android direct reply: the setting's length first, then 15 min / 30 min / 1 h / 2 h, plus typing; Mac text input, placeholder *30 min, 1 h, 2 h…*, empty = the setting); typed text goes through the shared duration parser (`45`, `1 h 30`, `1:30`, `2h`; 1–720 min); anything else changes nothing and the notification comes back silently. Remind less (es *Avisar menos*; was *Relax*) repeats the side every `max(repeatMin, relaxMin)`; it shows only when it would slow anything (no data, every 60 min, gets Mute… alone), and once relaxed the slot is **Remind as usual** (es *Avisar como siempre*); the interval is in the secondary text (*Reminders every 30 min*), never on the button. After Mute… Android replaces the alert with a silent *Low muted until 10:42* with **Unmute** and **Change…**; macOS removes a notification once an action is used and posts a passive one with the same two. Scenarios: `contracts/alerts/scenarios/actions.txt`. Android: the rules are the pure `:core:alerts` module (`AlertMachine`: inputs → effects Fire / Clear / Back / Mirror / Schedule; persisted `AlertState` incl. the wearer's shared run so a restart never re-rings followers; a restart alerts at once for whatever came due while the app was down; a follower's Mute / Unmute act on their side only — a high's Mute never hides a showing low; a clock set back keeps repeats, windows and mutes as far away as they were, and a reading dated in the future counts as taken now).
- Scope and end of Mute / Remind less (round f30; the user: "a high mute … then goes low, the low would be muted, and we don't want that"): they act on one side (lows incl. very low; highs incl. extremely high; falling fast; rising fast; no data), never the other. They cover very low and extremely high too (the user's decision; treatment mutes still never cover extremely high). They outlive the episode and end at their time (Mute) or once back in range **by the margin**: lows ≥ low threshold + 10 mg/dL, highs < high threshold − 10 mg/dL, for 10 min in a row (`notificationActions.backMgdl` / `backMin`, settings rows *Mute and Remind less end when back in range by* / *… for at least*); crossing back out restarts the count, so oscillation at the edge stays quiet. History: *Low back in range: mute and relax ended*.
- **Mute all** (round f30; the user: "a global mute button with a time option … Never is also an option"): Android home control **Mute alerts** (es *Silenciar alertas*), Mac dropdown **Mute Alerts**: 30 min · 1 h · 2 h · 4 h · 8 h · **Until you turn it off** (es *Hasta que lo desactives*, after AOSP Do Not Disturb; was *Never*) · Custom… (the one number input, 5 min–24 h, schema `muteAll`, whose special 0 is `until_turned_off`; was `never`). It quiets every kind, very low, extremely high and no data included, on that device only (the wearer still sends the followers' alerts); alerts showing go. When it ends whatever still matches alerts at once (history *Alerts back on*). It survives a restart.
- Notification actions and Mute all are per device (user 2026-10-21: "if someone wants to mute, it shouldn't mute the other"): only that device's notifications, pill and history change; nothing is sent. The wearer runs its rules twice (its own notifications with its actions, the followers' `alert` events without). On a follower mirroring the wearer (phone or Mac, `AlertMirror` on both) they drop the wearer's alerts there (while relaxed a repeat shows only after `relaxMin`; a more severe kind always shows) and end by the same time and margin rules on the readings that device gets. With own rules they act on that device's engine. Treatment mutes are shared data and quiet every device. Scenarios: `devices.txt` (`mirror`).
- Badge (`AlertStatus`, both platforms, `poc/spikes/graph-fixtures/status.json`): a side's Mute (or Mute all) out of range = the tonal pill with the bell and time left in the colour of what answers it (*Muted* when Mute all has no end); a treatment mute shows first. Mute all in range = the neutral **muted** pill with the "notifications off" bell, *Muted · 1h 20m* / *Alerts muted* (short *Muted*), on every card surface (app, widgets, lock screen, glucose notification, Mac dropdown and widgets); the Mac menu bar puts a bell.slash before the value.
- Durations (round f30; the user: "Showing 60 min and 120 min is long, 1 h and 2 h is better"): every duration in words goes through one formatter per platform (Android `core/Durations.kt`, Mac `Shared/Durations.swift`, cases `poc/spikes/durations/durations.json`): *45 min*, *1 h*, *1 h 30 min*; the card keeps its compact *1h 4m*. tools/check rule `durations` fails on a duration written by hand.

- Countdown and copy (round f31; the user, 2026-10-22: "muted alerts should show a countdown, and the Never option makes no sense … figure out the best copywriting"): every muted state counts down. Android's muted notification uses a count-down chronometer (`when` = the mute's end) titled *Low alerts muted* with *Until 10:42*; the pill, sheet, Active now and Mute-all line tick on each minute boundary; widgets and the lock screen re-render each minute while a mute runs. Mac rows, pill, Active now and menu-bar tooltip tick; Mac notifications say *Until 10:42* (they can't count down). The state lines come from one builder per platform (`MuteCopy`) tested against `poc/spikes/alerts/copy.json` (en + es): *Low alerts muted · 12 min left*, *Low reminders every 30 min*, *Alerts muted · 1 h 12 min left*, *Alerts muted until you turn it off*; choices *For 30 min* … *For 8 h*, *Until you turn it off* (AOSP DND's *Until you turn off* / *For 1 hour*); buttons *Unmute* / *Change time*, *Remind less* / *Remind as usual*; pill *Muted · 1h 12m* / *Muted*. Mac uses title-style buttons and menu items (*Change Time*, *Remind as Usual*, *Until You Turn It Off*). Round f33: the quiets after a treatment say it the same way and count down to the rule's end like a mute (*Low alerts muted after carbs · 12 min left*, *High alerts muted after insulin · 1 h left*; was *Low quiet after carbs until 10:42*), and the falling rule reads *High alerts muted while falling back into range*; the badge's screen-reader text (TalkBack on the widgets and the card, Mac VoiceOver) is the same line as the app's rows. Spanish agrees with the time left: *queda 1 h* / *queda 1 min*, *quedan 2 h* / *quedan 1 h 5 min* (Android `plurals` for every *queda(n) %s*: the mute lines, the pill *queda 1 h*, *Sensor: queda 1 d*, Sensor end's *Termina el … · queda 1 d*; the quantity is the duration's one number, `Durations.*Quantity`; `StringsParityTest` fails on a plain-string *quedan %s*). The Mac is English only (*left* doesn't change).
- **States and exits** (rounds f29, f30, f31; SPEC §2 "States and exits"; scenarios `actions.txt`, `exits.txt`, `devices.txt`; Android `AlertMuteTest`, `AlertExitsTest`, `MuteAllGallery`, `QuietGallery` read back the posted notifications; Mac `-alerts-selftest` notification checks on the real `Alerts` class). Every way out acts on this device only and updates history, badge, widgets and ring at once.
  - *Normal*: the alert notification (Mute…, Remind less), the *Eat carbs* / *Take insulin* pill. Out: the episode ends.
  - *Side muted (N left)*: Android the silent *Low muted until 10:42* notification with **Unmute** / **Change time** (es *Reactivar*, *Cambiar duración*); the tonal pill *Nm left* → sheet (**Unmute**, **Change to** 15 min / 30 min / 1 h / 2 h); Settings → Alerts → **Active now**. Mac: the passive notification with the same two, panel rows (Unmute, Change To), the pill's menu, Settings › Alerts › Active now. Out: Unmute (still out of range → alerts at once), its end (alerts at once if it still matches), back by the margin; Change = a new end from now.
  - *Quiet after carbs / rapid insulin*: the tonal pill and ring; the line *Low alerts muted after carbs · 12 min left* (ticks; Android home chip, sheet, Active now; Mac panel row, Active now); sheet / panel / Active now **Alert me anyway** (this device only). On a mirroring follower the wearer's phone decides (the sheet says *Low alerts muted after a treatment. The wearer's phone decides when they come back.*, *Alert settings*).
  - *Quiet: falling back into range*: Active now / panel **Alert me anyway**.
  - *Side relaxed*: Android chip under the card *Low reminders every 30 min* (es *Avisos de glucosa baja cada 30 min*), the notification's **Remind as usual**, sheet / Active now; Mac panel row, pill menu, Active now. Out: Remind as usual (an overdue repeat at once), back by the margin.
  - *Muted + relaxed*: both lines and both exits; the margin ends both.
  - *All alerts muted (time / Never)*: Android under the card *Alerts muted · 1 h 12 min left* (counting down) / *Alerts muted until you turn it off* (es *Alertas silenciadas · quedan 1 h 12 min* / *… hasta que lo desactives*) with **Unmute** (es *Reactivar*) and **Change**, the muted pill everywhere, Active now, TalkBack actions on the card (*Alerts: Unmute*, *Alerts: Change time*); Mac dropdown row with **Unmute** and **Change Time**, the menu bar's bell.slash (tooltip and VoiceOver *Alerts muted …*), the pill's menu, Settings › Alerts › Active now. Out: Unmute (what matches alerts at once), its end (never with Never), Change.
  - History lines (Android; the Mac logs them): Settings › Alerts › **Lately** lists the newest 5 of the last 24 h with their time (es *Últimamente*): *Low · 66 mg/dL* (an alert's first ring; repeats and silent re-posts after a restart aren't lines; *(from the wearer)* when mirrored), *High cleared* (the rules ended it, not a mute), *Low alerts muted for 30 min*, *Low alerts unmuted*, *Low alerts: remind less* / *remind as usual*, *Low back in range: alerts as usual*, *All alerts muted for 2 h* / *… until you turn it off*, *Alerts unmuted*. The runtime keeps the last 20 lines in the alert record (`AlertNotes`, saved with the rules' state, so a restart keeps them; *Start fresh* clears them). The group shows only while it has lines; it is read-only (the strips and pages above are the ways out).

- Falling-high mute (default 20 min): no high/very high alert while `sgv + rate × min < high`; it only postpones (a
  repeat that came due fires when it ends).
- Headlines: "Very low glucose", "Low glucose", "High glucose", "Very high glucose", "Extremely high glucose",
  "Falling fast", "Rising fast", "No glucose data"; body "69 mg/dL ↘ · < 70 mg/dL".
- Headlines acknowledge treatments logged in the episode (or within the mute length before it): lows "· eat more"
  (1 carbs entry) / "· eat more again" (2+); highs "· still high after insulin" / "· still high after more insulin"
  (never advise more insulin). Counted even when the mute is off.
- The wearer's `alert` event carries `tr` (that count) so followers and the Mac show the same headline.
- Notification body (Android + Mac): the value and arrow only (`69 mg/dL ↘`); with no reading behind the alert (Android) *No recent reading*, never a made-up value; falling / rising fast add the rate (`120 mg/dL ↓ · falling 2.4 mg/dL/min`); no data: `Nothing received for 20 min · last 120 mg/dL`. No threshold, no source ("from the wearer's phone").
- Android timer: exact alarm (`USE_EXACT_ALARM`) at the engine's next check, so a mute end alerts on time between
  readings; Mac: one-shot timer at the next check plus the 60 s tick.
- Badge status (`AlertStatus`): None / Needed / Taken / Paused use the same thresholds and treatment windows as the engine.
- Notification channels: two per kind. Android ids `alert.<kind>` (notification volume, `USAGE_NOTIFICATION_EVENT`, group *Glucose alerts*) and `alert_alarm.<kind>` (alarm volume, `USAGE_ALARM`, passes "Alarms" in Do Not Disturb and bypasses Priority mode once DND access is granted; group *Glucose alarms (alarm volume)*); the person sees them as *Very low*, *Low*, … under those two groups in System settings › Notifications. Ids never change (renaming resets the person's choices). (PoC: `alert_n_<kind>`, `alert_a_<kind>`.) Every alert that can ring posts as `CATEGORY_ALARM` so Android 15+ *notification cooldown* (per app: a second sounding notification within 60 s at 30 % volume, a third within 10 s muted; only alarms are exempt) never quietens one — Low and Falling fast fire in the same reading. A notification-volume alert keeps `CATEGORY_REMINDER` only while Do Not Disturb would treat the two differently (Alarms only, Priority without both alarms and reminders), so DND lets it through exactly as before (`AlertCategory`, `AlertCategoryTest`, `AlertNotificationAttributesTest`). Android also makes only one sound per app per second, so when one reading fires several alerts the one to hear is posted first (alarm level, then the most severe; the others show without sound; `AlertRingOrderTest`). Debug builds: `ALERTS --es kinds low,falling_fast --el gapMs 2000` posts made-up alerts through the notifier (docs/15 has the check).
- Android runtime (`:monitor` `AlertRuntime`): one alert runtime per process owns the engine; the monitoring service feeds it (hub state, settings, treatments) and the exact alarm (`AlertCheckReceiver`; boot, app update, clock and time-zone change through `BootReceiver`) ticks it **with the service dead**, from the saved record (episodes, mutes, Mute all, the last reading, the sensor's life, whether monitoring started): *no data* still alerts on time when Android killed the service, then the service is asked to restart. Both paths share the one runtime, so an alert never posts twice. Effects are applied before the state is saved (a crash in between rings again rather than resuming silently); each effect step (the alarm first, then notifications, followers, saving, publishing) is tried on its own, so a refused notification never leaves the exact alarm unarmed or the state unsaved; a failed step is written to the diagnostic log (no values) and listed in the hub's alert state as `AlertStepFailed` until it works again (no screen shows it yet: OPS issue); an alert Android refused to post is posted again, with its sound, on the next input. Boot, app update and clock changes check alerts and start the service once (`BootReceiver`). The alert check uses `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle`, not `setAlarmClock` (that would show an alarm icon and replace the person's wake-up alarm as the phone's "next alarm"); with exact alarms refused it falls back to an inexact while-idle alarm. **No phantom alarm**: before the first reading (or with no sensor / monitoring stopped) *no data* is off and nothing is scheduled; without a role nothing runs. Alerts are never bundled with other notifications: every alert and muted card is in Sukrara's own *Glucose alerts* notification group with its summary (Android 16 force-groups ungrouped and sparse groups; 0.11.53), the summary never rings and goes with its last alert. Alarm-volume kinds (very low and extremely high by default) also open over the lock screen with a full-screen intent when Android allows it; when notifications are off, an alert channel is blocked or full-screen isn't allowed, the hub's alert state lists the problem (`deliveryProblems`) for home and the reliability row. A swipe only removes that notification: the episode keeps its repeats.
- Android alert delivery (`:monitor`): every alert channel's sound is `content://app.mauri.sukrara.alertsound/<kind>` (`AlertSoundProvider`, exported read-only because System UI's player opens it): the file is picked at play time (the pack chosen in the app, a random variant), and a file that can't be opened falls back to the system sound, never silence; a sound picked in the channel's system settings wins. One vibration per kind (`AlertHaptics`, so the alert is recognisable in a pocket: lows fall and thud, highs rise, no data ticks away), composed from primitives, with an on/off waveform of the same rhythm where primitives aren't supported. The notification's actions follow the side's state: an alert whose repeat isn't faster than *Remind less* shows only *Mute…*; a relaxed side shows *Remind as usual* (with its "reminders every …" subtext). No per-side notification light colour (most phones have no LED).
- One sound per alert: each channel rings only its Sukrara sound; the app restores its episodes after a restart (an
  update kills it) and shows the alerts that were up again without sound; the next sound is the normal repeat
  (Android + Mac: Mac UserDefaults `alertEpisodes`, restored at launch with the last stored reading; history/log line
  "resumed High (no sound)"; scenario event `restart`, SPEC §7). Mac: the engine snapshot (open episodes, the sides'
  Mute / Remind less, Mute all; format `v` 1) is stored apart from the settings (SPEC §3 JSON under the schema key
  `alerts`); an unreadable or other-version snapshot starts fresh (alerts on, nothing muted), Mutes already over are
  dropped and Mutes longer than the longest choice are cut to it; a clock set back never stalls the repeats.
- Default sound pack: Quest, a different variant each time, no per-kind packs; followers mirror the wearer by default.

## Treatments

- Amounts are written lowercase with no space before the unit everywhere (Android + Mac): `4.5u`, `18u`, `30g` (dialog suffixes, lists, chart labels, the slot; also the Nightscout API's long-acting note; the Mac's treatment rows and amount field).
- Short durations read `now` (< 1 min), `12m`, `1h 5m` (whole hours: `3h`), only hours from 6 h (`7h`), days from 48 h (`2d`); time left rounds up (`1m` until it is over).
- The home treatments list (wearer) and the follower's list: each row starts with the time of day, then the amount as the card writes it and the kind (*30g carbs*, *4.5u rapid insulin*), and ends with the time since the entry in the card's short durations (`12m`, `3h`), refreshed on home's minute tick. Mac: Treatments window rows end with the time of day and the same `12m` / `3h` (refreshed every 30 s while shown).
- The treatment dialog (Android + Mac) focuses carbs instead of rapid insulin when the latest fresh reading is below an enabled low-side alert threshold (`AlertStatus.level` in `Kind.LOW_SIDE`); otherwise rapid.
- The treatment dialog on Android (home screen and `QuickTreatmentActivity`): the Rapid / Long / Carbs labels always sit small on the field outline on one line, with the unit (`u` / `g`) shown throughout, so no field and not the dialog changes height when the focus moves (`TreatmentDialogHeightTest`, en + es, 360 / 412 dp).
- The treatment dialog's time (Android + Mac): *When?* switches between a clock time and *Minutes ago* (Android: M3 segmented button, a number field with 5 / 15 / 30 / 60 min filter chips; Mac: segmented control, field + buttons). Minutes ago are 0–1439 (under a day, like the clock time; OK disabled when empty or 1440+, the field outlined red), 0 = now; the chip shows *16 min ago* (es *Hace 16 min*) and the entries get the time 16 min before the moment of saving, not of picking. The quiet line counts from that time. Cases in `poc/spikes/libreview/treatment-form.json` (`minutes`, `minute_filters`); `TreatmentTimeTest` (also at 411 dp).
- The treatment dialog's form (Android + Mac, `TreatmentForm`, cases in `poc/spikes/libreview/treatment-form.json`): insulin rounds to 0.1u (≤ 100u), carbs to whole grams (≤ 500g), `,` or `.` as decimal mark; 0, or 0 after rounding, is invalid; one invalid field blocks Save; a blank note adds nothing, a note is trimmed and clipped to 100 bytes.
- Writer scope (investigation 24; Android + Mac; `WriterScope`): a follower allowed to add entries (a writer) creates entries and changes or deletes **only the ones it created**; the wearer changes any. The wearer's phone refuses a writer's change to the wearer's own or another writer's entry (logged `treatment REJECTED: … may only change its own entries`; no echo, so the writer's copy stays *not accepted yet*). Followers apply the same rule: each entry's creator is the key that signed its first accepted version; a writer's copy that arrives after the wearer's echo of it is ignored, not counted as rejected; the wearer's own entry replaces a copy a writer forged first, whatever its version.
- **Lock-screen countdown** (Android, round 52, 0.11.52; `ui/QuickTreatmentActivity.kt`, `treatments/LockCountdown.kt`, `LockCountdownTest`): while the quick dialog is over the keyguard (*lock mode*: `KeyguardManager.isKeyguardLocked`, checked on every resume, so the lock widget, the glucose notification, the Quick Settings tile and a pen notification's *Add treatment* all count, swipe-only locks too) it closes by itself after *Time to save* (Settings → Lock screen, `lockTreatmentTime`, 10–120 s, default 20 s; raised to Android's *Time to take action* and to at least 60 s with TalkBack). The time counts from the dialog's first frame. Touches and typing never add time (in a pocket they type too). *Cancel* carries the countdown: a 24 dp ring that empties every frame with the seconds left inside it (the user asked to see the seconds count, 2026-10-25; the spec had a ring only), primary colour; it pulses once at 5 s, plus one light haptic tick if something was typed. The *When?* picker's and *Already logged by your pen?*'s Cancel show the same ring; timing out closes them with the dialog. Timeout: the dialog fades out and *Treatment not saved* is toasted only if something was typed (no haptic). The screen going off closes it at once, no toast. *Unlock* takes *Open Sukrara*'s place: the countdown pauses for the unlock prompt; success keeps the same dialog with every input (amounts, note, *When?*, the focused field and its keyboard), the ring shrinks away and *Open Sukrara* comes back; a cancelled or failed prompt resumes with at least 5 s. Unlocking on the lock screen itself (fingerprint) ends lock mode the same way. When the action row doesn't fit (Spanish at 360 dp) *Unlock* wraps to its own start-aligned line above Cancel / Save; no button is ellipsized. *Remove animations*: the ring steps once per second, no fill-in, no pulse (the tick stays). TalkBack: the digits are hidden; Cancel's state is *Closes in N seconds* in 5 s steps, one polite *Closes in 10 seconds*, *Unlock* reads *Unlock to keep editing*. The home screen's dialog never counts down.
- **States and exits**: *Treatment dialog* (home *Add treatment*, a card pill, a widget, the glucose notification, the Quick Settings tile, a pen read's *Add treatment*; Mac *Add Treatment…*) → *Save*, *Cancel* / back / tap outside / Escape (nothing saved, back where the user was). *When?* picker → *OK*, *Now* (back to now), *Cancel* (keeps the earlier choice). An invalid amount → the red limits line, *Save* disabled until it's fixed or cleared. *Already logged by your pen?* → *Cancel* (back with the values) / *Log anyway*. A phone that can't add entries → the app opens instead. Lock-screen countdown: *Lock mode* (the dialog over the keyguard, from the lock widget, the notification, the QS tile or a pen notification) → the countdown ring on *Cancel* → *Save* (saved without unlocking), *Cancel* / tap outside (nothing saved), *Unlock* (bouncer: success = the same dialog unlocked with everything kept, no countdown; cancel / failure = the countdown resumes with at least 5 s), timeout (closes, *Treatment not saved* if something was typed), screen off (closes). *Unlocked dialog* → *Open Sukrara*, *Cancel*, *Save*. *Time to save* → Settings → Lock screen → number dialog (*Cancel*; default 20 s among the presets 10 / 20 / 30 / 60, *Custom…* 10–120 s). TalkBack: Cancel's state *Closes in N seconds*, one announcement at 10 s, at least 60 s.
- Android (`apps/android/feature/treatments`): the quick dialog (`QuickTreatmentActivity`, opened by the glucose notification's action intent, `QuickTreatmentIntents.open` for widgets and the Quick Settings tile *Add treatment*) with Rapid / Long / Carbs, Note (Carbs focused when the latest fresh reading is under the low threshold, alert on or off,
or when the card's *Eat carbs* pill opened it), *Now* / *Earlier…* (*When*: *Time* picker or *Minutes ago* with 5 / 15

## Readings and backfill

- Wearer phone (`:sensor` `SourceManager`, the hub's one `ReadingSource`): readings come only from the Libre 3 (one live value a minute plus its 5-minute historic point, 24 h backfill on every connection) or Juggluco (history of 1600 entries at start, 180 after a reconnect, then its live stream; backoff 5 s → 60 s; entries marked `device: Sukrara` are refused so the app never re-reads its own values as fresh). Nothing is invented, repeated as new or extrapolated; values outside 39–501 mg/dL are dropped. Plain `http://` is allowed only to this phone (127.0.0.1 / localhost: Juggluco); every other connection is https / wss (`network_security_config`). The source is the setting `source.source` (Libre 3 directly by default, or Juggluco); a switch, a new pairing, *Try again* or Bluetooth coming back fully closes the old source before the new one starts.
- Android user-facing errors are sentences in the app's language (`ui/UserErrors`, round 22; `UserErrorsTest`), never an exception's class or a socket address: the home panel's and the source page's source line (*Can't reach Juggluco. Open it and check that its web server is on.*, *Juggluco stopped sending readings. Reconnecting…*, *Bluetooth is off*, *No sensor paired*, *The sensor has ended. Pair a new one.*, *This sensor isn't supported*, *Lost the sensor. Reconnecting…*, the Nearby devices permission, else *Can't get readings from the sensor. Retrying…*), and the failures of LibreView / Tidepool sign-in, the Nightscout test, settings export / import, CSV export, invite lookup and password-manager restore (network failures by kind: can't find the server / can't connect / didn't answer in time / secure connection failed; our own short messages as written; else *Something went wrong. Try again.*). The technical text stays in the log and the DebugReceiver state. The home panel shows the newest of the engine's and the source's reading and takes the current time again when the app comes back to the front (round 22: it once showed a 3-min-old value).
- Mac user-facing words never use protocol terms (v2, relay, roster, writer, epoch, BLE): the data source reads `Internet`, `Bluetooth · <phone>` or `Wi-Fi · Juggluco`; the follow state `Following Ana`; logs stay technical.
- Mac: every backfilled reading (v2 hour chunks over relays or Bluetooth, the BLE backlog, v1 Nostr, Juggluco's Wi-Fi history) goes into the 31-day readings store as it arrives, whatever its age; only the live 3 h working window is trimmed (a chunk older than 3 h before the newest reading used to be dropped, which left gaps in the graph after every launch, wake or reconnect).
- Mac follower (protocol v2): the chunk subscription starts 1 h before the newest stored reading, at least 13 h and at most 7 days back (the chunks' expiry); recomputed on every reconnect (wake, manual reconnect).
- Mac follower: 90 s after the chunk subscription starts (and again 90 s later) it checks the last 12 whole hours of the store; hours still empty while the stream is fresh (newest reading ≤ 15 min old) send one `history` request (`since` = the first empty hour) to the wearer over relays and Bluetooth, at most every 30 min (PROTOCOL-v2 "Missing history", as Android's follower).

- Android reads two sources (`:sensor`, `SensorProfile`): **FreeStyle Libre 3 / 3 Plus directly** (NFC start / takeover, then Bluetooth; 60 min warm-up and 15 d life when the patch info doesn't say; a sensor's own patch info wins) and **Juggluco's local web server** (Juggluco owns the sensor), plus five families **never tested on a real sensor** (`SensorProfile.verified = false`; decoding pinned by open-source recorded frames in `src/test/resources/traces`): **Dexcom G7 / ONE+** and **G7 15 Day / Stelo** (read along with the Dexcom app on a G7 this phone is bonded with, writing nothing to the sensor; 30 min warm-up, 10 / 15 d), **Dexcom G6 / ONE** (keys from the typed 6-character transmitter id; needs a session started by the Dexcom app or receiver; 2 h, 10 d), **Accu-Chek SmartGuide** (the standard Bluetooth CGM Service, bonded with the cap's PIN in Android's dialog; 60 min, 14 d) and **Aidex X / LinX / GlucoRx** (keys from the typed 10-character serial; 60 min, 15 d). These are found by short filtered scans (`ble/SensorScanner`: 30 s on, 60 s off, by service UUID or exact name, never unfiltered; Nearby devices covers it, no location), the found address is kept and later connections go straight to it over the same `GattLink` (indications, reads, write-without-response, a bond that waits 2 min for the PIN). After 10 empty scans the status says *not found* (check the id) and scanning goes on. Value rules (`brand/BrandDriver`): only the sensor's own number in its range (Dexcom and SmartGuide 40–400 mg/dL); below / above it the reading is the flagged **LO / HI** (`Reading.lo` / `Reading.hi`, stored and shared as 39 / 401 like Nightscout and xDrip+, no rate): the card says *LO* / *HI* in the range colour and the alerts treat it as beyond every threshold (see *Alerts*). The same for every family: Libre 3 (uncapped and capped value both below 40 → LO; above 400 → HI), Dexcom, SmartGuide (−∞ with its *below range* warning → LO, +∞ with *above range* → HI; NaN or a contradiction → no value), Aidex X. A field that isn't a measurement is no value, never a LO / HI: Dexcom 1–12 (old special codes) and the all-ones 0xFFF, Aidex outside 18–800 (Juggluco); stored backfill records keep only in-range numbers; timed by the sensor's own clock (G6 by the transmitter clock from its time reply, Aidex by a start that is kept once known); a value from the future is dropped, one older than 3 min is history, and the same value again after a restart is history (never the current value the alerts read). A wrong id / refused authentication / failed PIN closes the link (*Pair the sensor again*) and is kept with the pairing (`Pairing.failed`): a restart, reboot or Bluetooth toggle never retries it in the background (no PIN dialog out of nowhere), only a new pairing does. A Dexcom G7 that is already past its wear when this pairing first sees it (an old sensor still bonded, ended or in its grace period) is never adopted and never ends the new pairing: its address joins the pairing's skip list (`Pairing.skipAddresses`, at most 8; a new G7 pairing after an ended one also skips the ended sensor) and the scan goes on; Dexcom states other than *ok* give no value (calibration, temporary issue, sensor error); a Dexcom end state ends the sensor for good; a SmartGuide "session stopped" is confirmed with a status read and shown as *no session*, never as the end (its end is start + 14 d).
- The Libre 3 source (`sensor/libre3/Libre3Driver` over `sensor/ble/GattLink`): connect by the address from the NFC tap with autoConnect (no scanning), one GATT operation at a time. Protocol ported byte for byte from the PoC (handshake, fresh pairing after a scan, resume with the saved authorization, every notification subscribed, 120 s glucose wait after authorizing, 24 h backfill, warm-up wait), pinned by `Libre3DriverTest`. Reliability: every GATT operation and the service discovery time out after 10 s; a dropped or failed link is closed at once and a **new** connection is made after 1 s, doubling to 30 s while it keeps failing, back to 1 s once the sensor sends data; Bluetooth off closes everything with no retries (state *Bluetooth is off*), Bluetooth on reconnects at once with a new connection; callbacks of a closed connection are ignored; a revoked Nearby devices permission stops the link with a state that asks for it. No reading for 10 min on a running sensor (not warming up, not ended) restarts the link (`SourceManager` watchdog). After 3 authorization rejections in a row (the sensor's PIN / key changed: another app took it over) it stops reconnecting and says *another app has the sensor* (*Try again* / scan it again).
- NFC commands to the Libre 3 (`sensor/libre3/Libre3Nfc`) are retried up to 10 times within 5 s on a transient error or an answer with the error flag (Juggluco's `wholenfccmd`); a lost tag (phone moved away) aborts at once and the scan screen asks to try again.

## Sensor scan: other sensors

- **Android**: the Libre 3 receiver-ID rules below are in `:sensor`; the other sensors' scan is not included (see *Not included*). A Libre 1 / 2 tag shows *This isn't a Libre 3* with *Use Juggluco instead*.
- The New sensor screen's NFC tap checks the chip first (UID maker byte): only 0x7A (Abbott, Libre 3 / 3 Plus) gets Libre 3 commands. 0x07 (Texas Instruments: Libre 1 / 2 / 2 Plus) gets no command at all and shows *Libre 2 sensors aren't supported* — start it with Juggluco, then pick Juggluco as the glucose source — with a *Glucose source* button; any other tag → *Not supported* / "not a FreeStyle Libre 3".
- Receiver ID (`sensor/pairing/ReceiverIds`, `ReceiverIdPolicyTest`): a Libre 3 is bound for its whole life to the receiver ID it was started with; a takeover (`A8`) works only with that ID, a wrong one is refused (`B1`) and changes nothing. Starting a never-started sensor (patch state 1, `A0`) uses only the LibreView account's receiver ID (from *Sign in to LibreView*: LibreLinkUp login, read-only, stores the receiver ID and the email — never the password or a token), so Juggluco or Abbott's apps on the same account can take it over later; without it **nothing** is sent and the flow asks for the sign-in. A takeover tries the account's ID, then Sukrara's own (a random ID made once; it can't succeed in practice because Sukrara never starts a sensor with it); without the account ID nothing is sent either. Sign-out forgets the account's ID; Sukrara's own ID and the paired sensor stay. Every successful tap replaces the saved pairing (the sensor gives a new PIN) and the next Bluetooth connection pairs afresh.

## Root status bar (system icons)

- Android (`apps/android/monitor` `systemui/`; Settings → Status bar → *With root*): *Value in the status bar* (off by default; only someone who turns it on is asked for root) with a `slot:` placement (Placement: *With the system icons* = leftmost, *After the VPN icon* / *Bluetooth icon*, *Before the mobile signal* (the default: left of the more permanent mobile / Wi-Fi / battery), *After the mobile signal* / *Wi-Fi icon*) shows the reading as Android system icons, set by the root slot daemon (`SlotDaemon`, run with `su` through `app_process`, `IStatusBarService.setIcon`): two slots of its own, `sukrara_glucose` (the number, `sb_n…` / struck `sb_s…` / `sb_lo` / `sb_hi`) and `sukrara_glucose_trend` (the arrow every 5°, double from ±3, the ring, `sb_t…`); no readings / sensor ended = one icon in the trend slot (number slot removed); warming up = none. What they show is `GlucoseCard.face` for `status-icons` (`SlotIcons`, `SystemUiTest`). SystemUI draws and tints them; with the LSPosed module they get the range colour (out of range only; struck values keep SystemUI's tint). Their TalkBack text starts with *Sukrara glucose* (English: System UI reads it to recognise them), never matching another app's icons. The daemon never leaves a stale value: it clears its slots when it starts, when the app lets go or dies (EOF), when it crashes, and after 10 min without a word from the app (the app pings every minute and sends every reading and freshness step); it accepts only its own slots. The app talks to it on one background thread with timeouts (30 s for the root prompt, 5 s per reply), newest state only. A failed start (root refused, no answer) is retried after 5 min, doubling to 30 min (at once after a settings change or a System UI hello). The value never vanishes from the bar: while a `slot:` placement's icons aren't on screen (no root, the daemon failed) the module draws the text after the clock instead, and Sukrara's notification icon is hidden only while the value shows (text or icons). *Value in the status bar* stays switchable while on, so it can be turned off after root was revoked; root is asked only when it is turned on (and on the page while it is on).
- The LSPosed module (`SystemUiHook`, libxposed API 102, `META-INF/xposed`, scope System UI) draws the value as text after / before the clock or left of the system icons (the clock's font, tint and fading; range colour when on; the card's arrow or ring; struck at half strength from 5 min; the "no readings" icon from 20 min; the flag once ended; a warm-up's time left), keeps our icons ordered, coloured and first in line, and hides Sukrara's notification icon (*Hide Sukrara's notification icon*, on by default; the app re-posts its notification after the module's hello so it applies at once). It re-renders every 30 s by itself. It is fed by a broadcast only the app can send (signature permission + the sender's identity checked) with every value clamped. Safety: the whole entry and every hook catch everything (PROTECTIVE hooks; a failing piece turns itself off), at most 40 log lines a minute, kill switch `adb shell setprop debug.sukrara.systemui_off 1` (or `settings put global sukrara_systemui_off 1`) then restart System UI.
- **Glucose wins the space (LSPosed module, 2026-10-22).** Android 17 lays the icons out right to left and turns the first one that doesn't fit, and everything left of it, into one overflow dot; ours are external icons, so they were the leftmost and the number went first, leaving a lone arrow when vibrate / hotspot icons appeared. The module hooks SystemUI's `EllipsizingRow` measurement (`hook/SystemUiHook.hookIconFit`, logic in `hook/IconFit`, `IconFitTest`): SystemUI's own fit runs with our icons first in line, then the shown icons go back to their on-screen order. Other icons fold into SystemUI's dot (from the left, in SystemUI's order) before ours; number and arrow are one unit: both shown, or both inside the one dot (never one without the other) when even the pair alone doesn't fit. The placement only decides where they sit. Our icons are recognised by their TalkBack text (`RootStatusBar.isOurDescription`). If SystemUI's code doesn't match, the hook logs `icon fit … failed` and SystemUI's own behaviour stays.
- *Before the mobile signal* (`slot:<mobile`): right before the mobile icon; with mobile hidden (no SIM, Wi-Fi only), where it would be: after the last shown icon ranked before mobile in `config_statusBarIcons` (`IconFit.insertBefore`). On the user's Pixel that order is … speakerphone … vpn, bluetooth … mute, volume (vibrate), zen … hotspot, stacked_mobile, mobile, wifi, airplane, battery, so the pair lands after vibrate and never between mobile and Wi-Fi. Same on the lock screen status bar (the same icon list).
- **Without the module** (root only, or the module not in System UI's scope / not loaded, per `SystemAccess`): every `slot:` placement still shows the icons, as plain system icons first among them (SystemUI's own position, no priority, gap or order fixes); the Settings rows say so (*Before the mobile signal needs the LSPosed module. For now, it shows first among the system icons.*) and those placements stay selectable in the dialog with that line under them. The clock / left-of-icons placements show nothing without it, so they stay disabled with *Needs the Sukrara LSPosed module*. No root: the switch is unavailable, as before.
- "After X" while X is hidden (Bluetooth off, no VPN): SystemUI's list only has the shown icons, so ours go where X would be, by the platform's slot order (`config_statusBarIcons`): after the last shown icon that comes no later than X (`IconFit.insertAfter`). Before, they jumped to the far left.
- **Number and arrow at the card's gap (LSPosed module, 2026-10-22; the user: "closer, less spacing").** SystemUI puts its 6 dp icon spacing between our two slots (5.74 dp measured). The module brings the trend to the card's own number → arrow gap, so every surface reads alike: the renderer's geometry (`ValueGlyphs` `MARK_GAP` 0.08, `MARK_BOX` 0.62, `ARROW_IN_BOX` 1.05 of the text size, the arrow's outline) with the card's font, ink to ink, averaged over the digits and the 5° angles = 0.27 of the digits' height (`IconFit.CARD_GAP_OF_DIGIT`, recomputed by `IconFitTest`); at the status bar's 13 sp icons (digits 28 px at 420 dpi) that is 8 px ≈ 3 dp instead of 16 px. How: the measure hook makes the row that many px narrower and SystemUI's own placement block (hooked) moves the trend and everything after it left by as much, so the gaps to the neighbours and the dot stay SystemUI's; each icon is still placed once by SystemUI. Those px go to the other icons (2026-10-22; the user saw the dot where vibrate would fit): SystemUI's fit is offered the row's width plus the pull (its own measure Constraints, widened through Compose's `Constraints.copy`), so an icon that fits once the pair is closer shows instead of the dot. Ours are fitted first, so either both fit, side by side, and the pull takes the px back (the row ends within the real width), or they fold into the dot together and nothing else fits either (`IconFit.pulledRow`, a 20,000-row check in `IconFitTest`). Only when the number sits right before the trend in SystemUI's order (the order hook) and the gap hook is in place; SystemUI's fitting rules are otherwise unchanged. The trend slot's TalkBack text is *Glucose trend ↗* (the number's stays *Glucose 120 mg/dL ↗*), which is how the module tells them apart. Only left-to-right layouts, only when number and trend are shown side by side; anything unexpected → SystemUI's spacing (`icon fit … failed`). Root alone keeps SystemUI's spacing (nothing can move a system icon without the module).
- **After a System UI restart (2026-10-22; the user: the arrow showed on the left, then jumped).** system_server hands System UI our two icons back in hash order, trend first. With the module, the order hook always shows number then trend (`IconFit.arrange`), with every placement including the plain one and before the app's state arrives, so the pair is right from the first frame. The slot daemon's own fix (re-adding both icons 8–13 s after it sees a new System UI) was that visible jump and a remove / add blink, so it is skipped when the module said hello in that System UI with its order hook in place (the app sends `order module` to the daemon; keyed by System UI's pid, so a restart without the module re-adds again).
- **Root without the module**: after a System UI restart the arrow can show left of the number until the daemon re-adds them (8–13 s). The placement is always leftmost and SystemUI fits the row on its own, so a crowded row can still drop the number and keep the arrow. Not fixed with one combined icon per (value, trend): the Compose status bar ignores the icon level, so that needs 14,883 drawables (363 values × 40 marks + struck), measured +6.4 MB on the release APK.

## Sensor reminders and countdowns

Wearer only (`sensorend/`; rules in `SensorEndPlan`, tests `SensorEndPlanTest`); followers get none of these. Settings → **Sensor end**.
- **Android** (`:monitor` `sensorend/`: `SensorEndPlan` pure rules on virtual time, `SensorEndNotifier`,
  `AndroidSensorEndPresenter`; page `:feature:settings` `sensorend/SensorEndPage`; settings `sensor.reminders` JSON +
  `sensor.countdownHours`; tests `SensorEndPlanTest`, `SensorEndNotifierTest`). Differences from the description
  below: reminders and *Sensor ended* use channel `sensor`; both countdowns and *Sensor ready* (silent) use
  `sensor.countdown`; the sensor's start is derived from its end (15 d) or ready time (60 min); a sensor whose start
  moves by more than a day is a new one. Swiping a countdown away goes to `AlertCheckReceiver` (kept away for that
  sensor). States and exits: each reminder (tap opens the app, swipe); countdowns (swipe = gone for this sensor, or
  the switches on the page); *Sensor ended* (*Replace sensor* → new-sensor flow; a new sensor clears it); turning a
  switch off removes what it shows on the next re-plan.
- Reminders: a list of (days before the end day 0–3, time of day), at most 6; default *1 day before at 09:00* and *on the end day at 09:00*. Each fires in the phone's time zone on (end day − days) at its time, only if that is after the sensor's start and before its end (a sensor ending at 07:30 skips "end day 09:00"). Text: *Sensor ends today / tomorrow / on Wednesday at 14:30* (≥ 7 days: the date) · *Have a new sensor ready.* One notification slot: the later reminder replaces the earlier one.
- Reminders that passed before Sukrara knew them (new sensor, edited list) never fire; one missed while the phone was off fires late if the sensor hasn't ended (of several, only the latest).
- The page lists the reminders, then *Add reminder* as a button under the list (the same dialog, empty; hidden at 6), then the first-unlock switch in its own group.
- **First unlock** (switch *Remind at first unlock*, default on; 0.11.19): a reminder set for the morning (after 05:00, before 12:00) shows at the first unlock (`ACTION_USER_PRESENT`, registered at runtime by the wearer service; it can't be in the manifest) of its day from 05:00, if that comes before its time; otherwise at its time (the exact alarm stays as the fallback). The evening reminder below works the same way. An unlock counts only if it brings a reminder forward (`SensorEndPlan.unlockWanted`; other unlocks do nothing) and only if it is newer than the last reminder / the last settings change. The slot is remembered (`PlanMemory.lastReminder` = its time), so a later unlock, the fallback alarm or a restart never shows it twice; no alarm is left at its time.
- **Night-time end** (group *Night*: switch *Remind the evening before*, default on; *Bedtime* 23:00, *Wake-up time* 07:00, editable with the time picker; Android doesn't share its Bedtime schedule with other apps, so the window is Sukrara's own): if the end falls in the night window (bedtime inclusive, wake-up exclusive; it may wrap midnight), one more reminder at bedtime − 2 h (21:00) the evening before that night (for an end at 03:10 on Wednesday: Tuesday 21:00), or at the first unlock from bedtime − 4 h (19:00) if earlier: *Sensor ends tonight at 3:10* · *Change it before bed*. A night-time end means a gap in readings and a no-data alert during sleep. Same channel as the other reminders; not an alarm, not a Live Update (Live Update guide: "Inappropriate uses: … upcoming calendar events").
- **Last day, take the spare**: a reminder shown on the end day for an end later that day outside the night window says *Sensor ends today at 7:08 pm* (the phone's 12/24 h format) · *Take your spare sensor with you*; an end inside the night window keeps *Have a new sensor ready.* (the evening reminder covers it). Wording per the notification design guide (developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/home-screen/notifications): when it ends in the title, what to do in a short text that doesn't repeat it.
- End countdown (default on): an ongoing ProgressStyle notification from end − *Countdown starts* (`SensorLife.countdownHours`, 1–24 h, default 8, shared with the card's countdown ring) to the end: *Sensor ends at 14:30* · *Have a new sensor ready to replace it.*, the bar = time left in the window (empties; a plain bar, no tracker icon at its tip), a chronometer counting down to the end, no action buttons. Silent channel, refreshed every 15 min (non-waking).
- It is promoted (Live Update chip with the timer) only while the glucose chip is off: the status bar shows one chip and the countdown would take it from the glucose value for hours. With the glucose chip on it stays in the shade and lock screen. On Android 16 without QPR2 (API 36.0, no promoted notifications) both countdowns are plain ongoing notifications (the promotion calls are tried, never required; `SensorEndNotifyTest` posts both on Robolectric's 36.0).
- Warm-up countdown (default on): from a new sensor's start to the warm-up's end (`SensorInfo.warmupEndEpoch`, 60 min), always promoted: *Sensor warming up* · *Readings at 19:45*, the bar fills up (`SensorLife.warmupFraction`), the chip counts down; refreshed every 5 min. **A countdown never goes below zero**: at the warm-up's end (exact alarm) it becomes *Almost ready* · *Waiting for the first reading*, bar full, no timer, until the first reading; it gives up 15 min after the warm-up end without a reading (`SensorLife.WARMUP_GRACE_S`). The first reading cancels it (in any state) and posts its own *Sensor ready* · *Readings have started.* (sound, gone after 10 min), only if the warm-up countdown was shown and within 1 h of that reading. The first reading's time is remembered per sensor, so a restart (an app update) doesn't bring the countdown back; every re-plan without a warm-up cancels a leftover one. The end countdown likewise stops at the end (exact alarm) and hands over to *Sensor ended*.
- No *Hide* button on either countdown (0.11.20: the user wants no Hide button on any notification). The way out: **swipe it away** (since Android 14 an ongoing notification is dismissible except while the phone is locked and on *Clear all*, developer.android.com/about/versions/14/behavior-changes-all; a Live Update can be "temporarily dismiss[ed] or demote[d] … to a standard notification", developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/live-update), which keeps it away for that sensor through the delete intent (the guide: "Don't repost Live Updates that the user dismissed. Use setDeleteIntent to detect dismissed updates"), the warm-up one then also skipping *Sensor ready*; or **turn it off**: Settings → Sensor end → *End countdown* / *Warm-up countdown*, the system's *Sensor countdowns* channel, or Live Updates for Sukrara in the system settings (`ACTION_MANAGE_APP_PROMOTED_NOTIFICATIONS`).
- At the end (or when the sensor reports it ended early, end moved to now): *Sensor ended* with the action *Replace sensor* (tap or action: the New sensor screen; the card's wording) once, on the reminders channel, at most 12 h after the end; a new sensor clears it.
- Channels: *Sensor reminders* (default importance, sound) for reminders / ended / ready; *Sensor countdowns* (default importance so it shows on the lock screen, no sound or vibration) for both countdowns. Not the alert channels.
- Scheduling: one AlarmManager alarm at a time for the next moment that matters, re-planned on every alarm, new sensor or end change, settings change, boot, app update, clock and time-zone change. Reminders, window start, warm-up end, warm-up give-up and end: `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle` (Sukrara already holds `USE_EXACT_ALARM` for alerts; 10-min window if exact alarms are unavailable); progress refreshes: non-waking `setWindow` (≤ 1 min late), used only when they land before the next event even if late, so a late tick never runs a countdown past its end. The first reading re-plans at once (while the warm-up may be up), and a failing re-plan doesn't stop that watch.
- Settings export / import / reset include the reminders, the first-unlock and evening switches, the night window, both countdown switches and the countdown hours.
- Debug (DebugReceiver, adb only): `--es cmd sensorEnd` without extras only reads the state (nothing changes); it takes the settings `--ez unlock B`, `--ez evening B`, `--es night 'MIN-MIN'`. Debug builds only (`DebugFakes`; a release build changes nothing and says `debug builds only, nothing changed`): `sensorEnd --ei inMin N` (a made-up sensor), `sensorStart`, `sensorUnlock` (an unlock), `fakeSensor`, `sensor`, `backfill`, `alertFeed`, `alertTest` (a test alert), `previewSgv` (a status-bar preview), `locktest`, `newSensor --es stage`, `treat` (an adb treatment): the release app never shows a glucose value, an alert, a treatment or a sensor state that isn't real. Guarded by `DebugFakesTest` and the `tools/check` rule `debug` (0.11.20, after `cmd sensorEnd` on a release build replaced the real sensor).

## Sensor end in calendar

- **Not included**: no calendar events; the *Sensor ends in …* / *Sensor ended* notifications are the reminder.
- Setting "Sensor end in calendar" (wearer only, off by default; Settings → Sensor end, its own group): turning it on asks for READ_CALENDAR + WRITE_CALENDAR; refused → stays off with "needs access to your calendar"; refused for good → the next tap opens the app's system settings. Permission revoked later → the setting turns itself off.
- Summary: "On · <calendar>" or "Off".
- Default calendar: Sukrara's own **"Sukrara"** calendar (`ACCOUNT_TYPE_LOCAL`, account "Sukrara", brand colour #1BB5C9, read-only for the user), created when needed and found by account (never twice). It shows in the phone's calendar apps but is **not synced** to other devices (local calendars never sync; an app can't create a calendar in a Google account without the Google Calendar API).
- **Google Calendar hides it by default** (seen 2026-10-18 on the user's Pixel, Google Calendar 2026.39, with the event correctly in the provider): Google Calendar shows a non-Google account's calendars only after the user turns the account on under Google Calendar → Settings → Manage accounts → "Non-Google Accounts" → Sukrara (On this device), and that switch starts off. Its decompiled code (`ica.b`) keeps the state in its private prefs as `account:<name>:type:<type>|account_enabled_state`; with no stored state the account is hidden (Exchange excepted), so Sukrara can't turn it on. Hence the hint row below.
- "Show in Google Calendar" row (only while on, with Sukrara's own calendar, and when Google Calendar `com.google.android.calendar` is installed; manifest `<queries>`): "Turn on Sukrara in Google Calendar → Settings → Manage accounts, or the events won't show there"; tapping it opens Google Calendar (its launch intent; no deep link to that page is known).
- "Calendar" row: pick Sukrara's own calendar or any writable calendar on the phone (e.g. the user's Google calendar, "syncs to your other devices"); events in a picked calendar are written as a normal app so the account syncs them, and their ids are remembered for updates. Changing the choice moves the events (removes them from the old place).
- One event per sensor: title "Sensor ends", description "Libre sensor <serial>, started <date>. Sukrara keeps this event up to date.", 30 minutes long from the sensor's end (SensorLife), busy = free, no reminder of its own (Sukrara notifies). Not a point event: Google Calendar drew start = end as a title-less sliver. No RSVP: Google Calendar read the event out as "Response: Not responded" (its `SELF_ATTENDEE_STATUS` was 0). Setting `ORGANIZER` = the calendar's `OWNER_ACCOUNT` ("Sukrara" for the own calendar, read from the picked calendar otherwise) with `HAS_ATTENDEE_DATA` 1 was not enough (verified on the phone), and the provider refuses a direct `SELF_ATTENDEE_STATUS` update. So each event also gets exactly one Attendees row for that owner (email and name = owner, accepted, relationship organizer, type required; sync-adapter URI for the own calendar, plain URI for a picked one): the provider then sets `SELF_ATTENDEE_STATUS` 1 and Google Calendar reads "Sensor ends, 7:08 pm – 7:38 pm, Sukrara" with no guest list or RSVP. Every write checks all of Sukrara's events in the target calendar (past ones too) and inserts the row only when none with that email exists (never a second one); skipped when the calendar has no owner account. ORGANIZER and HAS_ATTENDEE_DATA stay set. The current sensor's existing event gets the 30 minutes on the next write while it is still ahead (a past event keeps its start and end). Keyed by serial (`_SYNC_ID` "sensor:<serial>" in the own calendar), so restarts and repeated updates update it instead of adding another.
- When the sensor ends early (SensorLife pulls its end to now) a still-future event moves to the real end; once an event's time has passed it never moves again (start and end stay).
- Changing the calendar carries only the current sensor's event to the new place.
- Past sensors' events stay (a history of sensor changes); a sensor's event still in the future is deleted once another sensor is the current one (replaced early, that end won't come).
- Turning it off deletes the Sukrara calendar with all its events, or Sukrara's events in the picked calendar (as deletions the account syncs).
- Only the wearer's app writes events; followers never do (no serial on the wire).
- Research and options weighed (Juggluco's ACTION_INSERT, local vs Google account vs picked calendar vs Google Calendar API): `features/SensorCalendar.kt` header.

## Sharing over Bluetooth

- **Wearer** (Android, Settings › Sharing): a *Bluetooth* group with *Share over Bluetooth* (on by default; schema
  `sharing.bleFollowers`). Its line says what holds: *2 devices connected nearby*, *On · no follower device nearby*,
  *On once someone follows you* (the phone advertises only while it has a follower, B§3), *Bluetooth is off* (row *Turn
  on Bluetooth* → Android's Bluetooth settings), *Needs the Nearby devices permission* (row *Allow Nearby devices* →
  the permission dialog for Bluetooth advertise + connect), *This phone can't share over Bluetooth*, or *Off. Followers
  get readings over the internet.* The page looks again on resume and after the permission answer.
- While the switch is on, *Nearby devices* lists the follower devices connected now (*Bea · phone* / *Sam · Mac*, their
  picture, *Connected over Bluetooth*). A connection is named only by the identity proof in its `HELLO`
  (poc/spec/ogs-ble.md B§5/B§6): the follower signs a kind-21080 event with content `H("cgm2:ble-id:"+sid+":"+nonce+":"+dev)`
  with its follower key, and the wearer's phone checks it against each active follower and device type
  (`HelloProof.identify`; vector `ble.hello_id`). An unnamed link is not listed (no made-up name); a replayed `HELLO`
  names nothing. A row opens the follower's dialog; *Remove* there also drops that follower's Bluetooth links
  (`OwnerEngine.forget`), and a device that connects again is neither named nor trusted.
- The wearer's phone (`AndroidNearbyServer`, protocol in `NearbyServerCore`) advertises the Sukrara service connectable
  with **no name** (no scan response), opens a new GATT server after Bluetooth comes back, and stops with a visible state
  when the permission is revoked (never a crash). It pushes every event it publishes except the owner backup and the
  invite wrap, which stay on the relays; live and alert pushes go ahead of a long backlog. Events a follower device writes
  are trusted only by their signatures: gift wraps to the identity are verified before they count as seen (requests,
  rekeys, history requests), a writer's entry goes through the writer rules and is forwarded to the other devices.
- **Follower** (Android, `AndroidNearbyCentral` + `NearbyLink`; Mac `NearbyCentral`): while following, Bluetooth runs
  beside the relays; copies are one reading (deduplicated by event id, one reading source). The Following page says
  *Bluetooth: connected to their phone* only once the phone's `INFO` proved it's this wearer's; otherwise *looking for
  their phone*, *Bluetooth is off* (*Turn on Bluetooth*), *Needs the Nearby devices permission* (*Allow Nearby devices*:
  scan + connect, `neverForLocation`) or *not connected* (monitoring stopped).
- `HELLO` carries a fresh random nonce; the phone's `INFO` answers `m = H("cgm2:ble:"+sid+":"+nonce)`. A follower writes
  nothing to a phone (requests, rekeys, history requests, treatments) until its `INFO` proves our stream (`m` over one
  of its last 4 nonces; once the follower knows the stream id a pairing `i` never proves it). Another wearer's phone is
  avoided for 10 minutes (that phone only, by its current Bluetooth address / Mac peripheral id: never the one connected
  since; a phone that rotates its address sooner can be tried again) and the scan goes on; on
  the Mac, a phone with nothing to pair with (not following yet) is left for a minute. A pending request: receive only.
  After a new key the follower sends `HELLO` again with its proof. Entries still pending go to the phone as soon as it
  proved it's the wearer's; one counts as delivered on a relay's OK or the wearer's echo (a bare write response doesn't).
- Reliability (Android follower): scan by service UUID (starts spaced: at most 4 per 30 s; restarted every 25 min), one
  connection at a time with a 25 s connect timeout (that phone skipped 60 s), MTU 517, a 10 s timeout on every GATT
  step, `HELLO` again after 3 min without data, link dropped after 5 min, rescan after 3 s doubling to 1 min while
  connections fail, a new `BluetoothGatt` per connection.
- Frame sizes: every frame fits one ATT write / notification at the link's MTU (Android central: the negotiated MTU;
  Mac: CoreBluetooth's write-without-response length + 3), so a Mac at the default MTU 23 needs no long write; the
  wearer's phone still accepts a long (Prepare/Execute) write as one frame (`contracts/protocol/ble/default-mtu.json`).
- Limits: reassembly drops a message idle for 30 s or over 256 KiB, at most 16 open at once (Android and Mac). The
  wearer's phone takes at most 8 follower devices at once (a connection counts only once it writes to the service), checks
  each write before answering (a malformed frame, a long write out of offset order or over 512 bytes, or more than
  256 KiB a minute → error and disconnect;
  more than 30 messages a minute → dropped), sends one backlog per device per 5 s (a sooner `HELLO` is answered when due),
  and never re-sends an expired event from its backlog.
- **Pair nearby** (Android wearer, Settings › Sharing › Bluetooth, shown while Bluetooth can serve): a switch that opens
  a single-use invite for 10 minutes (`OwnerEngine.openNearbyPairing`; no link and no invite wrap on the relays, the
  backup records the token). While open the phone advertises even with no follower and its `INFO` carries `i`, `relays`
  and `pair` (token, expiry, the wearer's profile; B§7.3); the summary counts down (*Open · closes in 9:41*). It closes
  at the first request with it, after 10 minutes, with the switch (*Stop*), when *Share over Bluetooth* is turned off
  or when sharing stops or the app restarts; each drops the unclaimed invite (a restore keeps an invite nearby). A request made
  with it is marked nearby: the approve dialog says anyone in Bluetooth range could have asked and Allow needs the code
  typed from the follower's phone (never compare-only), and its notification has no Allow (*Open Sukrara and type the
  code*).
- **Find phones nearby** (Android follower, setup's join step, under the link field): looks over Bluetooth while the list
  shows (`FollowerEngine.findNearby`): each phone found is connected in turn, sent a `HELLO` without a proof, its `INFO`
  read and the phone left (nothing else is written: with no identity every phone is "unknown", B§6): a phone offering
  nothing for a minute, one offering for 5 s. Phones with an open, unexpired `pair` are listed with the wearer's
  picture and name (by invite); one that stops offering leaves the list at its next read, one that stops answering
  after 12 s. Scans share the app's scan-start budget with the follow link (Android allows 5 starts per 30 s). A follow that ended never asks again over Bluetooth. A tap joins with that offer (no relay lookup)
  as the name typed above; the follow's own Bluetooth link then sends the request to the phone whose `INFO` names that
  identity, and to the relays. The join step shows the match code as for a link. The Mac follower does the same from its
  follow window.
- Mac follower: gift wraps (access request, rekey, history request) carry an absolute expiration (7 d; history 1 d); a key
  delivery naming another stream id is rejected; a chunk whose `c`/`d` tags don't match its hour is rejected; a rekey
  request goes out only for an unknown key on an event from the wearer's identity or device.

**States and exits**: wearer switch on/off (the switch itself); Bluetooth off → *Turn on Bluetooth* (Android settings,

## Tidepool upload (docs/27)

- Settings → Share with apps → *Your clinic* → *Tidepool* (both roles; Android only, the Mac doesn't upload;

## LibreView upload

- Settings → Share with apps → *Your clinic* → *LibreView* (wearer only; Android only, the Mac doesn't upload;
  `:integrations` `libreview/`: `LibreViewClient`, `LibreViewQueue`, `LibreViewUploader`, `LibreViewPage`; tests
  `LibreViewPayloadTest` over `contracts/treatments/libreview-fixture.json`, `LibreViewUploaderTest` against
  MockWebServer). **Off by default**; the Share with apps row shows the status and the overview summary includes
  `LibreView` while it is on.
- *Account* opens *Sign in to LibreView* (email, prefilled from the sensor screens' LibreView account; password, "Used
  once to sign in, never saved"). The LibreView app login (`/api/nisperson/getauthentication`, Abbott's config URLs
  checked to be https) gets a token; the token and the profile fields the sensor registration repeats are kept only in
  the Keystore-encrypted secure store (`libreview`); the password never leaves the dialog's memory and that request.
  Wrong password: "LibreView didn't accept this email and password". If another device uploads for the account
  (LibreLink, the Libre 3 app, Juggluco), the dialog says so and offers *Take over* (`SetDevice:true`, that device stops
  uploading) or *Cancel*: Sukrara never takes over by itself.
- *Upload to LibreView* (enabled once signed in; turning it off always works, also after signing out or on a follower
  phone, which sees "Only the phone that wears the sensor uploads"), *Status* ("Last upload 4 min ago · 3 items", "On ·
  nothing sent yet", "Trying again soon · <reason>", "LibreView asks you to sign in again (another app may have taken
  over the account)" — tapping it opens the sign-in).
- *What to send*: *Glucose* ("From a Libre 3 connected to this phone, since <date time>") and *Insulin, carbs and
  notes* (entries made or changed from now on; edits and deletes correct what LibreView has); both on, disabled while
  the upload is off. Each part goes up only from the moment it (or the upload) was switched on: the person's earlier
  LibreView data (from LibreLink or another app) is never doubled. *Sign out* (confirmed) forgets the token; LibreView
  keeps what it has.
- What goes up: exactly what the FreeStyle Libre 3 app sends (contracts/treatments/SPEC.md §3, byte for byte). Entries:
  one record per version (`recordNumber` from the entry id and version); an edit sends a retraction of the version
  LibreView has plus the new one, a delete only the retraction, one deleted before it went up nothing. What LibreView
  got is remembered for a year (typed store `libreview-upload`), so later edits still retract it; entries made on
  another device wait 2 min. Glucose: only readings Sukrara takes from a Libre 3 itself (the sensor link tags each
  batch with its serial; Juggluco's readings never go up), record number = the serial's base | life count; the newest
  reading as current (< 5 min old), the 5-minute history, `sensorstart` once per sensor after `PUT /api/nisperson`
  registered it (skipped if sign-in didn't return the profile, so it can't blank it). Unsent history is kept in memory
  (3 days): readings lost to a restart come back with the sensor's backfill.
- When: after each new Libre 3 reading, entry change or setting change, and every 5 minutes. At most 100 entries and
  288 readings per upload; a bigger catch-up continues at once. Network or server trouble retries after 30 s doubling
  to 15 min (±20 %); a token LibreView no longer accepts (401 / 403 / `wrongDeviceInToken`) stops until the person
  signs in again (unlike the contract's re-auth: the password isn't kept); entries LibreView refuses (`status` ≠ 0)
  wait until an entry or a setting changes (glucose goes on without them), refused glucose retries with the backoff,
  a refused sensor registration holds that sensor's glucose until the next sign-in; the reason is in the status. An
  entry whose kind changed is retracted under the kind LibreView has. Redirects are never followed.

- Home's treatment list (wearer, while the upload is signed in and on with *Insulin, carbs and notes*): a small grey
  cloud after each entry the upload covers, by the uploader's own rules (`LibreViewQueue.mark`): cloud with a tick =
  "In LibreView" (the version LibreView has), cloud with an arrow = "Not in LibreView yet" (a new entry) or "LibreView
  has an older version: updating" (edited), crossed-out cloud = "LibreView upload stopped" (sign in again, or LibreView
  refused the last upload; tapping it opens the LibreView page). Entries changed before the upload was switched on, or
  older than 30 days, get none. Deleting an entry LibreView has while the upload runs (not stopped) says "Treatment deleted. It will be removed from LibreView
  too." in the Undo snackbar; Undo sends it up again as a new record (a retracted entry has no live record to retract).

**States and exits**: *Signed out* → *Account* → dialog (*Cancel* keeps nothing). *Another device uploads* → *Take
over* or *Cancel*. *Off* (default) → main switch. *On · nothing sent yet / last upload* → main switch off, *Sign out*.
*Trying again* → retries by itself; switch off to stop. *Sign in again* → the status row or *Account*. *Refused* → change
or switch off. *Follower phone* → switch shown off and disabled with the reason. Back leaves the page.

## Nightscout upload (docs/25)

- Settings → Share with apps → *Nightscout* → *Nightscout upload* (wearer only; Android only, the Mac doesn't upload;
  `:integrations` `NightscoutUploader`, `NightscoutPage`; `NightscoutUploaderTest` against MockWebServer). **Off by
  default**; the Share with apps row shows the status line and the overview summary includes `Nightscout upload` while
  it is on.
- *Site* opens a dialog: address, *Access token* or *API secret*, the token / secret field (empty keeps the saved one of
  the same kind for the same address: a saved token is never sent to a new address), *Test* (read-only: the site's status with that credential; "Connected · Nightscout <version>", "The
  site refused this token or secret", "Couldn't reach the site (…)") and *Save*. The token or secret lives only in the
  Keystore-encrypted secure store (`nightscout`), never in settings, logs or backups, and is never shown again.
- Addresses: `https://` with a certificate from a public authority (user-installed / private CAs aren't trusted, so a
  self-signed home server fails *Test* with a TLS error); `http://` only to this phone (`localhost`, `127.0.0.1`),
  the only cleartext the network security config allows. Any other `http://`, home network included, shows "Use
  https://: Sukrara sends your data only encrypted" (issue filed: a home-network `http://` site needs a network-config
  and local-network-permission decision). A redirect is never followed (it could carry the secret elsewhere): it shows as
  a retry with "redirected, check the address".
- *Upload to my Nightscout* (enabled once a site is saved; a follower phone sees "Only the phone that wears the sensor
  uploads"), *Status* ("Last upload 3 min ago · 12 items", "On · nothing sent yet", "Trying again soon · <reason>",
  "The site refused the token or secret: change it" — tapping it opens the site dialog —, "Paused: another app
  (<device>) uploads to this site" with *Upload anyway*).
- *What to send*: *Glucose readings* (every reading, the last 30 days included), *Insulin, carbs and notes* (edits and
  deletions too), *Sensor changes*, *Phone battery*; all on, disabled while the upload is off. *Advanced*: *API* (v1
  recommended; v3 only with an access token, a secret keeps v1) and *Remove this site* (confirmation; forgets the site,
  the credential and what was sent; the site keeps its data).
- What goes up: only readings this phone took from its own sensor (Libre 3 or Juggluco; never a followed person's or
  test data): `sgv`, integer `date`, `dateString` (local on v1, UTC on v3), `utcOffset`, `direction` or `NONE`, 5-minute
  `delta`, `noise` 1, `app` `Sukrara`, `device` this install's own name `Sukrara-<6 hex>` (chosen when the site is set,
  so another app or another install is never mistaken for this one), Nightscout's v5 identifier; rapid insulin as
  `Correction Bolus`, carbs as `Carb Correction`, notes as `Note`, long-acting as a `Note` with the dose only in `notes`;
  a new sensor as `Sensor Change` + `Sensor Start`; the battery as devicestatus when it changes or every 30 min.
- When: a cycle at start, after each new reading, entry change or setting change, and every 5 minutes (never in the
  reading path). v1 sends 500 readings per request; v3 one per request, at most 240 per cycle.
- Catch-up and duplicates: what the site lacks is planned from the phone's store every cycle; on the first run, after
  failures and every 6 h the phone compares with the site's readings: minutes the site lost are sent again, minutes
  within 2 min of another uploader's reading count as covered (a backfill never doubles someone else's data), and
  another device's readings in the last 30 min **pause everything** (readings, entries, sensor, battery) until *Upload
  anyway*. Failures retry after 30 s doubling to 15 min (±20 %, or the site's Retry-After); 401 / 403 stops until the
  token is changed. Upload only: nothing is read back except those reading dates.

**States and exits**: *Not set up* → *Site* → dialog (Cancel keeps nothing). *Off* (default) → main switch. *On ·
nothing sent yet / last upload* → main switch off, *Remove this site*. *Trying again* → retries by itself; switch off to
stop. *Token refused* → the status row or *Site* opens the dialog; saving a new token resumes. *Paused (another
uploader)* → *Upload anyway*, or switch off. *Follower phone* → the switch is disabled with the reason. Back leaves the
page.

## Languages

- English (default) and Spanish (Spain) everywhere, settings pages included (every page, alert page, dialog, search and the alerts page's "lately" list); the phone's language picks one, or Settings → Apps → Sukrara → Language (per-app language, Android 13+).
- Settings search finds rows in the shown language, accents optional ("insulina rapida" finds "Tras insulina rápida"): the index is built from the pages as they are drawn.
- Settings words follow Android's own Spanish where a stock setting exists: Ajustes, Activado / Desactivado, Pantalla de bloqueo, Barra de estado, No molestar, Restablecer, Buscar en Ajustes.
- Text lives in `res/values/strings.xml` and `res/values-es/strings.xml`; a unit test fails when a key or a format argument is missing in a translation.
- Numbers, dates and times follow the chosen language (decimal comma in Spanish, localized day and month names); units stay as they are (mg/dL, `4.5u`, `30g`).
- Every clock time follows the device's 12/24-hour setting, not the language's default (Android: Settings → System → Date & time → *Use 24-hour format*, read live; Mac: System Settings → Date & Time → *24-hour time*): `21:08` / `9:08 PM` (es `9:08 p. m.`). That covers the card (`Ready at`, *No reading since*, a late reading's time), the graph's hour labels, widgets, lock screen widget, the live notification, sensor reminders and countdowns, the calendar event's description, treatments (list, dialog, followers' entries), alerts list, access requests, settings summaries (reminder times, sensor end, LibreView), the Mac menubar tooltip, dropdown, report and treatments. One helper per platform (Android `graph/Clock.kt`, Mac `Clock` in SukraraGraph.swift), and it is the only place the setting is read (Android: `Clock.hour24Source`, installed by the app; Mac: `Clock.hour24`, formatting with the bare locale so the cycle asked for is the one shown): no surface passes a 24-hour flag of its own (2026-10-21: the value widget showed `08:59` beside the chart cards' `8:59 AM`). Shared cases in `poc/spikes/graph-fixtures/clock`; every surface is rendered in 12- and 24-hour time by the cross-surface tests (`clock12` / `clock24`: every time any surface formats or draws must be in that cycle); `tools/check` `clock` rejects an own flag or time formatter. Switching the setting (or the time zone) while Sukrara runs redraws everything at once: Android's role receivers take Settings' `ACTION_TIME_CHANGED` / `ACTION_TIMEZONE_CHANGED` and re-render the notification and the widgets, and `Clock.signature()` is in every redraw key (widgets, notification, its pictures; `ClockRefreshTest`); the Mac redraws the menubar and dropdown and re-publishes to the widgets on the current locale's change and the time zone's (`Clock.observeChanges`, `-graphselftest`). The log and debug output keep 24 h.
- Spanish terms: hidratos (carbs), insulina rápida / lenta, tiempo en rango, seguidores; alert headlines like "Glucosa baja · come más".
- Changing the language (the phone's, or Sukrara's own per-app language) renames the notification channels and groups (the person's channel settings stay) and redraws the glucose notification at once; the next alert is worded in the new language (Android: `App.onConfigurationChanged` → `MonitorModule.onLocaleChanged`). Widgets follow on their next redraw.
- Alert rules and their timeline scenarios stay in English: the engine reports the kind and what was logged, and the notification and the settings pages word them. The log and the debug dump stay in English.
